# Cardio + Yoga Protocol — Where Things Stand

*Plain-language synthesis of the 8/12–8/13 paired sessions, the rules that came out of them, and the month-long test block. PT-facing version to be exported separately.*

---

## The Two Sessions

**Cardio (stair stepper, 8/12).** This is the threshold-setting tool. 12:50, stopped by symptoms, under the week-1 duration target. Symptoms first appeared at HR 115; PT set the working ceiling at 114. Two things about this session carry forward: I kept going past symptom onset instead of stopping, and I logged *that* symptoms appeared but not *how much* they climbed. The protocol defines the threshold by a rated +2–3 increase, not by first noticing something. So **114 is a cautious ceiling my PT drew, not yet a real established threshold.** The month block is what turns it into one.

**Yoga (Phase 3 full block stack, 8/13).** 38:36 — roughly three times the cardio duration for an equivalent next-day cost (pain +1, neuro +2). Symptoms surfaced at HR 108, but caffeine within the hour before means that number can't be trusted or reused. Six confounding factors total. This session counts as proof I can *tolerate* the full stack — not as a threshold measurement.

## What the Pair Established

**1. The full yoga stack is tolerable at this duration.** One instance, messy conditions — but the session completed with a small next-day tax instead of the unusable post-state the elliptical produces. Three times the workout for the same cost is the number the choose-yoga decision rests on. The month block tests whether it holds.

**2. The heart recovers before the nervous system does — and my data caught it.** The descending yoga blocks brought heart rate down exactly as designed (−48 over the back half), and symptoms did not follow it down. This matches well-documented exercise science: heart rate drops within minutes after effort, but full nervous-system recovery lags by hours to days. **Plain version: a calm heart rate is not proof the system has cleared.** The restorative back half of the sequence is doing real cardiac work — it is not evidence the autonomic state resolved. This finding survived every confound in the session.

**3. The 24-hour spacing makes the pair one unit.** My symptoms lag activity by 6–48 hours, so yoga ran inside cardio's delayed window. With this spacing, the two sessions are one combined test every time — the state I bring into yoga is always partly a cardio after-effect. That's acceptable as long as it's held constant and labeled honestly. The cost: I can't attribute a next-day tax to yoga alone this month.

---

## Governing Rule — Decided with PT

**The autonomic/neuro track (0–5) is the real-time stopping signal. Pain (0–10) is a lagging indicator.**

Why: my pain runs on a 6–48 hour delay — it reports what already happened, not what's happening. The autonomic symptoms (dizziness, spatial, etc.) are the only signal that arrives *during* load. So:

- The +2–3 stopping criterion reads against the **neuro scale**, always.
- Pain still gets captured at the same moment — as context, never as the stop signal.
- The two scales are allowed to disagree. They measure different clocks.
- At any re-test, the heart rate that matters is the HR at the **neuro +2** moment. If pain moves first, that's data, but it doesn't stop the test.

*Status: provider-confirmed with PT, August 2026. Upgraded from internal working rule.*

## New Observation — Right-Side Real-Time Ping (Provisional)

Self-observed across multiple sessions: at peak heart rate, I usually feel a structural/muscular pain signal from an area already flared or activated that week — and it's almost always right-side.

**Why this doesn't break the "pain is delayed" rule:** baseline tissue stays quiet until the delay window. But tissue that's *already sensitized* has a lowered firing threshold — it's the one part of the pain system capable of reporting in real time. Flared tissue pings live; everything else reports late.

**Why right-side makes sense:** the right side has carried triple duty for 20+ years — its own job, coverage for the post-surgical left shoulder, and backup position-sense for the neck. Under peak demand, it's the scaffolding the system leans on hardest, so it signals first. It's also the same structure whose release work produced the autonomic symptoms last spring. Both signals — the autonomic constellation and the right-side ping — may be two outputs of the same event: demand hitting the compensatory chain's limit.

**Status: provisional.** Tracked during the month block at no extra cost — when logging pain at the neuro +2 moment, tag the location and whether that tissue was already elevated that week. If the right-side ping reliably arrives with or before the neuro +2 across four weeks, that goes to PT as a possible second real-time marker.

## Month Block — The Rules

Same cardio→yoga flow, weekly, four repeats. The full stack counts as one unit, so this gives n=4 on the unit — not on any individual block. For the four instances to pool, hold constant:

1. **No caffeine within ~4 hours of either session.** Caffeine shifts heart rate and stress response enough to make any HR number unusable.
2. **Med state consistent across weeks** — and stated in each entry, whatever it is.
3. **Cardio capture:** HR at the rated neuro +2–3 moment, then stop. No taper, no pushing past.
4. **Cardio→yoga spacing fixed** at whatever this month uses; note it once per pair.
5. **Entry state logged before each session, not after** — both ratings, both sessions.

Five capture fields per session. Everything finer waits for the wearable.

## The One-Paragraph Version

Cardio sets the threshold; yoga is the therapy that runs three times longer for the same cost. The autonomic symptoms are the only real-time gauge I have, so they govern when to stop — pain reports the past, not the present. Heart rate recovering is not the same as the nervous system recovering, and my sequence design does the first without guaranteeing the second. Four clean weekly repeats under held-constant conditions turn all of this from single observations into usable numbers.
