# Weekly Experiment Log — W03 · April 27 – May 3, 2026

**Week descriptor:** A needling-heavy PT session landing on a sleep deficit produced a four-day escalation to L2, then a two-day settle that returned PT exercises — and a Saturday yoga session that seeded a full autonomic attack on Sunday.

---

## 1. TL;DR

Monday was ten hours of client work and extended screen, and the body scan was the lowest of the week — lower trap, brachial, and lumbar all at low band, and no autonomic at all. Whatever the previous week's recovery built, it held under a heavy load.

Tuesday was PT, and the session was the opposite of the week before: needling on the suboccipitals and C5–C6, a lot of soft tissue work, no exercises, and no proprioceptive work. It landed on a short night, after a night where the log notes mood was depressed about the neck. Pain climbed through the afternoon and peaked. Suboccipitals and temporalis both hit top of band.

Wednesday was a protected day, planned in advance and held. It still went sideways: preparing for Thursday's call re-engaged the screen in the afternoon and triggered autonomic symptoms plus nausea while the suboccipitals were still reactive. Ice on the suboccipitals alone was not enough once screen load was added — a qualification of the rule she'd written the week before. Gentle stretching was attempted and didn't get far.

Thursday was the peak. Upper trap at top of band, a new high. She hadn't been able to side-sleep the night before and had to lie on her back. Extended screen, a video call, and long conversations holding space for friends. Full autonomic constellation with nausea most of the day. She cancelled Friday's planned social event that evening.

Friday settled. Pain at mid-band, no autonomic, brachial at clear, thoracic erectors at low band, no pain meds needed — and PT exercises came back, the first since before the needling session. The log marks it: return of generative interventions.

Saturday looked good and cost something. Coffee with a friend for an hour and a half was tolerated. A twenty-minute yoga sequence produced dizziness on the chaturanga floor-to-standing transition, amplified by going straight to screen work afterward. Animated conversation with friends at the dog park and at coffee produced flare pains — logged as consistent with cervical proprioceptive load.

Sunday it came due. Residual autonomic depletion from the yoga session, plus morning vibration work on the suboccipitals and temporalis, tipped into a full autonomic attack — the first one in the record. Forty-five minutes horizontal with a calming intervention to come back online. She cancelled the drive. She goes into Tuesday's PT without a real recovery day, and the log says so.
