# Weekly Experiment Log — W08 · June 1–7, 2026

**Week descriptor:** The first week in the record where every provider ask was acted on — deep soft tissue directly on the cramping knot and a reduced-coverage tape config, both requested in the brief — across a week with no brain fog on any day, one flare that forced her out of a coffee shop, and a drive protocol she designed herself and never got to test.

## 1. TL;DR

Monday she woke clean and the morning walk cleared the scapular pangs outright — second time in two weeks that a morning walk has done that. Then a heavy screen day, two hours at a coffee shop, groceries, four hours on the couch, and client work running into the evening. The throb came back by early evening. The body scan logged levator scap with a laterality split — left low, right above baseline — the first time laterality has been split inside a scan field.

Tuesday was PT, and both things the brief asked for happened. Deep soft tissue on the right side, starting with the cramping knot that's been spasming since the pre-PT walk late W07, then up into the usual upper trap knots. And the tape went back on in a **simpler neck-and-traps-only configuration** — a direct response to the brief flagging skin reactivity and the cramp correlation before any re-tape. That's two asks, two answers, in a record where the previous two briefs got none.

Wednesday she woke with no real soreness anywhere and the day ran at baseline with heavy but low-stress screen work and zero autonomic.

Thursday reversed it. Flare onset around hour three at the floor desk, an animated hour-long call at noon, then a coffee shop she had to leave after about an hour. The **left scalene fired** — first bilateral activation in a week where the right side had been the whole story — and it stayed local, no referral. Spaciness lingered into the evening with a queasy stomach and no appetite. The log is careful to note the autonomic ramp co-timed with a wellness support intervention coming on, so those two can't be separated.

Friday isn't logged.

Saturday the mid-spine pinch came up during errands — rhomboids and lower traps close to the spine, elevated pain in that one spot, and she names it as the area that's been a problem since January. Then several hours poolside with her head turned to talk to a neighbor, tagged as sustained cervical rotation. She taped her own neck that evening.

Sunday it was gone, and the day was emotionally stressful rather than physical. She took a pain management intervention specifically to head off symptom onset from the emotional overwhelm — treating sympathetic load as a predictable trigger rather than waiting to see.

No brain fog on any day this week. Exhaustion, named on six of the previous eight logged days, doesn't appear once.
