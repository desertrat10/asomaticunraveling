## 1. TL;DR

The week answered two questions and opened a third.

Monday tested how long the first K-tape cycle held after removal. About eighteen hours: SCM and scalenes reactivated on the morning dog walk, and a mid-afternoon trigger flare came up in the mid-right thoracic. Pain was 6, against 4 on the last day the tape was on. She also put in fifteen minutes of trap-targeted weights — the first direct strength work on that cluster since the tape went on.

Tuesday was PT: soft tissue, no needling, and a fresh tape application. Her own words in the log: *less pain post-PT than the past month — K-tape helping.* First time she'd attributed pain reduction to the tape directly.

Then six days with no autonomic symptoms at all, at pain 5 to 6. Wednesday was a recovery day and the scan broadly improved, including both of Tuesday's 4s. Thursday brought a 75-minute animated meeting — a named trigger — and it did produce a felt physical shift afterward, but she caught it early with a THC microdose and a second four hours later, and nothing surfaced. The log is careful about this: that clean day was intervention-supported, not tape alone.

Friday she wrote from the floor meditation chair instead of the floor desk and carried heavy groceries. She flagged the ergonomic deviation at midday as the suspect; the end-of-day scan confirmed it, with SCM and scalenes both hitting 4 — cervical-specific, nothing thoracic or lumbar. Pain back to 6.

Saturday she dropped the cleaning and the work and kept only the four-hour mentor coffee, moving and changing position throughout. The cervical cluster *improved* across a four-hour social block. She also logged something new and precise about the overnight pain: stiff under the tape, cramping below it.

Sunday broke the streak. A thirty-minute drive on a familiar suburban route, no stress, clean morning baseline — queasiness started about twenty minutes in, then vision changes, an unsettled stomach that needed ginger ale through the meeting, and cognitive hiccups where she couldn't hold conversational threads. A pain microdose before the drive home reduced the intensity but didn't prevent it recurring. The tape was on the whole time and did not buffer it.

Cumulative therapy fatigue — roughly eight weeks of it — was named as a baseline factor three days running, independent of what the week actually demanded.
