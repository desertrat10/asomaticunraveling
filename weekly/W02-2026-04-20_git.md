# Weekly Experiment Log — W02 · April 20–26, 2026

**Week descriptor:** A six-day recalibration arc off a substitute-PT session run without needling — autonomic escalating through day 3, first documented dual-task degradation on the travel day, then a clean recovery to a med-free Sunday that included a 1.5-hour coffeeshop session with no reaction.

## 1. TL;DR

The first fully logged week, and the most informative one in this set.

Monday was PT with a substitute practitioner, and needling was declined by request — the direct answer to last week's question about spacing. The session was proprioceptor work, manual fascial release on the right traps and scapular region, an autonomic exercise using a laser headlamp against a wall target, and ulnar nerve work. Autonomic symptoms came on about four hours later and escalated through the evening. Nausea appeared in the post-PT cluster for the first time. A hydrocodone microdose cleared the lightheadedness, the nausea, and restored appetite — one dose, multiple symptoms, and the log marks it as the second data point for that effect. Couch-bound by evening, pain 7, flare L2.

Tuesday the scan dropped hard — temporalis and SCM both from 4 to 1. A long morning meeting at the floor desk ran with "no issues, lots of movement."

Wednesday undid it. A 1.5-hour coffeeshop session and extended horizontal-rig typing beyond normal duration, on a short night from working late, on recalibration day 2. Autonomic escalated after the coffeeshop and she had to lie down on getting home. Bilateral ulnar nerve irritation appeared, attributed to the typing. That evening she wrote down an intervention rule: ice on the upper traps and suboccipitals when autonomic symptoms are active — vasoconstriction to stabilize local blood pressure, cold to slow nerve conduction and reduce erroneous afferent signal — as a peripheral analog to what the hydrocodone microdose does centrally. Heat for purely muscular, ice when autonomic is in the mix.

Thursday, day 3, autonomic came on at two and a half hours of floor desk and stayed all day; meloxicam and a microdose reduced it without resolving it. The cycle started that day, nine days early.

Friday was the functional-capacity test: drive to Boulder, an hour of seated podcast recording, and manual gear setup. She couldn't hold a conversational thread while setting up gear — the first documented instance of dual-task motor/cognitive degradation. She detected the closing window, skipped the happy hour she'd planned to attend, and drove home with margin. The log includes a driving load taxonomy and a contrast pair: same route six days earlier, no autonomic response. The variable isn't driving; it's stacking sustained-position transit with cognitive and motor load.

Saturday was the best autonomic day of the week despite unplanned physical work. Sunday had no autonomic at all and needed no meds — the first genuinely med-free day. And Sunday included a 1.5-hour coffeeshop session with no reaction, against Wednesday's identical duration that produced a crash.
