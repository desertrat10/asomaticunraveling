# Working Thesis v2: Sensorimotor Dysfunction in Multi-Level Cervical Degenerative Disc Disease

*A Somatic Unraveling — Field Notes from the Cremation Grounds*

---

## Core Thesis

Cervical degenerative disc disease spanning five or more vertebral segments (C2–T1) produces a qualitatively different pattern of sensorimotor dysfunction than the whiplash-derived model (Treleaven 2008) predicts — one characterized by distributed proprioceptive corruption, layered compensation architectures, and a self-reinforcing sympathetic-proprioceptive feedback loop that elevates baseline autonomic tone independent of psychological stressors. The resulting reduction in **autonomic headroom** — the system's remaining capacity to absorb additional sympathetic load before crossing into symptomatic disruption — is the primary mechanism governing symptom onset, flare trajectory, and therapeutic targeting in this population.

The existing sensorimotor literature, built almost exclusively on whiplash-associated disorder (WAD) cohorts with acute traumatic onset and single- or two-level involvement, does not account for the distribution, chronicity, or compensatory complexity of multi-level degenerative presentations. This thesis proposes that four features distinguish the multi-level DDD sensorimotor profile from the WAD model and require separate investigation.

## The Four Novelty Claims

### 1. Compensation-Release Destabilization

When decades of pre-existing compensation (from prior surgery, asymmetric occupational loading, or athletic training patterns) have already reorganized the sensorimotor map before cervical degeneration begins, the central nervous system has fewer adaptive pathways available. In this case profile, the nervous system's compensatory strategy upweighted contralateral muscle spindle data — from a scapular complex carrying 20+ years of compensation for a prior surgical deficit — as substitute proprioceptive input for degraded cervical mechanoreceptor signaling. When PT releases this tissue (the clinically appropriate intervention), it produces autonomic responses disproportionate to the local tissue work, because the tissue being released carries backup proprioceptive responsibility for the entire cervical spine.

This is not a standard post-PT soreness pattern. It is **compensation-release destabilization**: the deliberate dismantling of a proprioceptive workaround the nervous system built over decades, before the replacement system (endurance-based thoracic and scapular stabilization) is fully online. The autonomic symptoms (lightheadedness, blurred vision, spatial disorientation, nausea) are the expected cost of the transition, not a sign of clinical error.

**Longitudinal evidence (W01–W19):** PT sessions consistently produce L1–L2 flare responses with autonomic constellation onset at 4–24 hours post-session. The W01 needling cascade (96-hour arc, bilateral brachial involvement by +96h, release toolkit non-responsive at +48h) and the W02 substitute-PT response (L2 flare without needling, nausea entering the autonomic cluster for the first time) demonstrate that the autonomic cost is driven by compensation release, not by the specific modality applied. Lower-back spasms (E1–E10 across W01–W19) emerged as right-side bracing released and load redistributed down the posterior chain — the compensation architecture unwinding inferiorly.

### 2. The 6–48 Hour Symptom Lag

Multi-level cervical DDD compromises the proprioceptive system that would normally report cumulative load in real time. Symptom onset is delayed, not concurrent with the triggering activity. In the longitudinal dataset, the lag ranges from same-day (4–6 hours for autonomic onset post-PT) to 72–96 hours (peak flare from dry needling cascade in W01). The median latency for PT-related flare peaks is approximately 24–48 hours; for activity-stacking events (coffeeshop seating, travel, cardio at elevated baseline), same-day to next-day.

This lag is not a peculiarity of individual sensitivity. It is a structural consequence of degraded mechanoreceptor afferents across six cervical levels: the error signal accumulates before the mismatch crosses the threshold into symptomatic disruption, and the crossing point is governed by available autonomic headroom at the time of the triggering event. The same activity (1.5-hour coffeeshop session) produces autonomic crash when stacked on an open recalibration window (W02, day 2 post-PT) and no reaction when performed at baseline (W02, day 6). The variable is the headroom, not the exposure.

**Clinical implication:** "I feel fine right now" is not data about the next 48 hours. External structure (timers, scheduled stops, logged load tracking) replaces internal sensation as the primary regulatory mechanism. Any management framework that relies on real-time self-report for load titration is operating on corrupt data.

### 3. Trained Proprioceptive History Unreliability

In bodies with elite-level proprioceptive training (D1 collegiate athletics, 15+ years of yoga practice with advanced certification), the internal feedback system has been conditioned into high sensitivity and high trust. When that system becomes unreliable due to cervical degeneration, the mismatch between trained proprioceptive confidence and actual signal degradation produces a distinct clinical pattern: the patient is more likely to override external structure because the internal signal "feels" authoritative even when it is corrupt.

This is not denial or noncompliance. It is a trained response — decades of athletic and somatic practice in which attending to and trusting body signals was the correct strategy. The degeneration has changed the hardware; the software (the learned pattern of trusting proprioceptive input during absorption/flow states) remains active. Timer protocols in the daily structure compensate for both: degraded hardware AND active software override.

**Longitudinal evidence:** The W02 dual-task degradation event (travel day, recalibration day 4) is the clearest demonstration. The patient could not simultaneously talk and set up gear — mapped to cervical proprioceptive bandwidth competition between spatial orientation, motor precision, and verbal processing. Real-time load detection and early exit from the social event preserved driving margin. The detection itself was a trained capacity; the fact that it had to be deployed indicates the proprioceptive system was already past its reporting threshold without the patient's conscious awareness.

### 4. Multi-Level Disc Distribution Dynamics

When afferent corruption is distributed across six cervical levels rather than concentrated at one or two (the WAD model), the question is whether the sensory mismatch behaves as an aggregate (sum of individual-level deficits) or as a system-level disruption with emergent properties the single-level model does not predict.

The longitudinal data suggests the latter. The body scan tracking across W01–W19 shows that flare responses do not localize to the most structurally compromised segments (severe C6–C7, severe left C5–C6 foraminal narrowing). Instead, they propagate through the full cervical-thoracic-lumbar chain: upper traps and suboccipitals at +24h, brachial plexus at +48h, bilateral involvement by +72–96h, lumbar spasms emerging weeks into the PT round as compensation patterns release superiorly and load redistributes inferiorly. The W19 rotation spike — catching/rubbing/grating at the cervical discs on active right rotation, new as a logged descriptor, going bilateral within 24 hours — demonstrates a presentation that implicates the full disc column, not a single level.

The distribution also affects therapeutic targeting. Standard cervical rehabilitation protocols designed for focal rehabilitation (single-level disc, post-surgical, or WAD) address individual structural levels or muscle groups in isolation. A distributed multi-level presentation requires intervention across the full kinetic chain — scapular positioning, thoracic extension, cervical alignment, hip stability — simultaneously. This is the mechanistic argument for yoga therapy as a system-level intervention for a system-level dysfunction.

## The Autonomic Headroom Model

The central integrating concept across all four novelty claims is **autonomic headroom**: the system's remaining capacity to absorb additional sympathetic load before crossing the threshold into symptomatic autonomic disruption.

Cervical DDD produces structural proprioceptive dysfunction — degraded mechanoreceptor afferents generating erroneous signaling that creates sensory mismatch between cervical proprioceptive, vestibular, and visual systems (Li 2022, building on Treleaven 2008). This mismatch drives tonic sympathetic activation through the vestibulo-sympathetic reflex pathway and reticular formation. The activation is structural, not psychological: it cannot be down-regulated by relaxation, mindfulness, or stress reduction alone, because the error signal originates in damaged disc tissue, not in cognitive appraisal.

The structural sympathetic elevation reduces the available buffer zone. Additional load — a PT session, a coffeeshop visit, a travel day, a cardio protocol, hormonal fluctuation, sleep deficit — stacks on an already-elevated baseline. The crossing point into symptomatic disruption (lightheadedness, blurred vision, nausea, spatial disorientation, brain fog) depends on the gap between current sympathetic tone and the individual's threshold, not on the absolute magnitude of any single load event.

This model explains the otherwise puzzling variability in the longitudinal dataset: identical exposures produce dramatically different outcomes depending on where the patient sits in a recalibration window, how many load events have stacked in the preceding 48 hours, and whether the autonomic buffer has been replenished by genuine rest or depleted by invisible accumulation.

**Therapeutic implication:** Yoga therapy — specifically, sequences designed to activate parasympathetic pathways through breath regulation, restorative postures, and slow proprioceptive sequencing — does not address the structural source of dysafferentation. It lowers the ambient sympathetic floor, expanding the autonomic buffer zone available before symptom onset. This is a mechanistically distinct intervention from PT's structural endurance target: PT builds the capacity the compromised system needs; yoga therapy creates the autonomic margin the system needs to tolerate the building.

## Relationship to the Literature

### Treleaven 2008 — The Foundation

Treleaven's sensorimotor model remains the most coherent framework for explaining how cervical spine pathology produces dizziness, autonomic disruption, and postural instability. Her core thesis — degraded cervical proprioceptive afferents create a sensory mismatch between cervical, vestibular, and visual systems, producing measurable disturbances in joint position sense, eye movement control, and postural stability — is fully consistent with this case profile. The autonomic symptoms (lightheadedness, nausea, blurred vision, spatial disorientation) follow via the reticular formation and vestibulo-sympathetic reflex pathway exactly as her model predicts.

What Treleaven's model does not account for is the **distribution** (six levels vs. one or two), the **etiology** (degenerative vs. traumatic), the **compensatory complexity** (decades of pre-existing reorganization), or the **trained proprioceptive history** (elite athletic and somatic conditioning). This thesis extends her framework to a population her empirical base was not designed to describe.

### Li et al. 2022 — The Mechanistic Bridge

Li, Yang, Dai & Peng (2022) provide the mechanistic specification for how cervicogenic dizziness arises from sensory mismatch — abnormal cervical proprioceptive input (from trauma, muscle spasm/fatigue, or degenerative disease) conflicting with vestibular and visual input during multilevel CNS integration. Their explicit inclusion of degenerative disease as a causal pathway (alongside trauma and muscle dysfunction) is the literature's clearest acknowledgment that the mechanism operates beyond whiplash. The autonomic symptoms follow via the vestibulo-sympathetic reflex pathway; the treatment recommendation (manual therapy as most widely supported) aligns with the structural-layer intervention in the dual-track model proposed here.

### Key Differentiation from Existing Literature

The four-domain intersection — yoga therapy × confirmed multi-level cervical DDD × proprioceptive outcomes × autonomic outcomes — has not been studied. The yoga-for-neck-pain literature (Li et al. 2019 meta-analysis; Jain et al. 2021 cervical spondylosis) measures pain and disability endpoints without proprioceptive or autonomic measures. The sensorimotor training literature (Treleaven 2017; Röijezon et al. 2015) uses exercise prescriptions targeting individual deficits rather than integrated system-level interventions. The autonomic-cervical literature (Moustafa et al. 2021; De Hertogh et al. 2025) documents the mechanism without testing yoga therapy as an intervention pathway.

## Proposed Investigation

### Design

Single-Case Experimental Design (SCED), A-B-A with concurrent B1 (yoga therapy) + B2 (athletic PT). The concurrent design reflects clinical reality: there is no equipoise for withholding manual therapy during a proprioceptive-autonomic intervention in a patient with active multi-level DDD.

### Primary Outcome Measures

- **Proprioceptive:** Cervical Joint Position Error (JPE), Smooth Pursuit Neck Torsion Test (SPNT), postural sway / Overall Stability Index
- **Autonomic:** Heart rate variability (HRV, high-frequency power component), resting heart rate trend, autonomic symptom frequency and severity from HealingOS daily structured logging

### Secondary Measures

- Daily pain tracking (NRS)
- Multi-region body scan (12-group, 0–5 scale, directional)
- Autonomic symptom constellation tracking (lightheadedness, blurred vision, spatial disorientation, brain fog, nausea — binary presence + severity)
- Flare frequency, severity classification (L1/L2/L3), and recovery window duration
- Recovery-windows longitudinal dataset (days from load event to return-to-baseline)
- Reactive-to-generative ratio

### Novel Data Infrastructure

HealingOS — a patient-sovereign longitudinal health tracking system — provides continuous daily structured data across the full study period and extending months before and after. This is not retrospective chart review or periodic assessment: it is real-time patient-generated data captured at the point of experience, with documented protocol versioning, data integrity tracking, and carry-forward continuity across weeks. The dataset includes 180+ daily logs and 19 weekly experiment logs as of October 2026, with recovery-window extraction, flare classification, and intervention-response tracking built into the logging protocol.

## What This Is and What It Is Not

This is a working thesis — v2 of the investigative framework for "A Somatic Unraveling: Field Notes from the Cremation Grounds." It synthesizes six months of longitudinal self-experimentation data with the existing sensorimotor and autonomic literature to articulate what the data shows and where it diverges from the models the field has built.

It is not a treatment recommendation. It is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for the clinical judgment of the care team managing this condition. It is a single case (n=1) with no external validity claims. All findings are specific to one body, one condition, one set of protocols.

What it is: a patient-practitioner's attempt to make the data legible to the researchers and clinicians who might find the question worth investigating — and a public record, under CC BY-SA 4.0, of what it looks like when the body's own reporting system can no longer be trusted and the person living in it builds the external infrastructure to compensate.
