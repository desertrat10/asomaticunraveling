# Weekly Experiment Log — W13 · July 6–12, 2026

**Week descriptor:** The lowest pain range in the set — 3 to 5 all week — and two clean findings inside it: skipping the pre-emptive afternoon dose produced a spasm cascade, and the first in-person event in two months located a capacity ceiling that has nothing to do with duration.

---

## 1. TL;DR

Pain never went above 5 this week. That's the flattest, lowest week in the record, and it happened with no PT for twenty-five days.

Monday was a neuro hangover from Sunday's composting-and-laser stack, compounded by very poor air quality from smoke. She slept ten or eleven hours, woke tired but clear-headed, then got completely wiped out mid-morning — lucky, as she puts it, that the morning meetings had rescheduled. Waves of energy and kriyas through the afternoon.

Wednesday she took a pre-emptive microdose at lunch specifically to head off autonomic symptoms before errands, and nothing surfaced at all — on a heavy screen day with an acute stress spike from last-minute tech changes.

Thursday was the week's best day, pain 3–4 and flagged baseline. She ran the full pre-load protocol before a ninety-minute workshop facilitation — horizontal rig to bank headroom, yin yoga, microdose forty-five minutes ahead, golden milk with lion's mane — and it held. Then she did three minutes of laser PT exercises afterward, and set an explicit tracking flag: monitoring the 24–48 hour window given the 7/3 → 7/4 laser hangover pattern.

**The window landed.** Friday, twenty-four hours out, the day was relatively pain-free until a cascade came on strong and suddenly — pains starting poolside from sustained head-turning, then right levator scap stabbing in the evening, then a lower back spasm about thirty minutes after that. Stretching didn't touch it. And she caught something else about that day herself: **it was the first day without an early-afternoon pain med, and the cascade happened in the absence of that usual pre-emptive dose.** That's a natural experiment on the protocol she'd been running all week.

Sunday was the first in-person event in two months — Factland in Boulder, thirty people, outdoors, 95°F, about six hours door to door. The pre-load held for roughly the first ninety minutes. And the finding is precise: **the real capacity ceiling isn't event duration — it's dense multi-directional standing conversation at close range.** That phase ran about forty-five minutes before a visual-proprioceptive cliff — visual spots during close-range conversation, losing her train of thought, nervous system activation from a close talker. Blurred vision at home, brief nausea on arrival, complete exhaustion. Her words: like I ran a marathon.

She tags it as a **new trigger profile, distinct from the bar-and-TV mismatch** — this one is social-spatial, generated by the interaction pattern rather than the venue.

One quieter thing worth noting: a full week of consistent deep sleep since reintroducing the sleep edible.
