# Beyond Whiplash: Sensorimotor Dysfunction in Multi-Level Cervical Degenerative Disc Disease

*A research premise, experimental question, and proposed investigative framework — revised after Li et al. (2022)*

---

## Version Note

v1 identified a gap in Treleaven's whiplash-derived sensorimotor model and proposed a single-case experimental design to probe it. Between May and October 2026, two things happened that forced a revision: a clinically significant adverse event (documented in `neuro-storm-case-report.md`) and the discovery of Li et al. ## The Gap (v1 — unchanged)

Julia Treleaven's sensorimotor model (2008) is the most coherent framework available for explaining how cervical spine pathology produces dizziness, autonomic disruption, and postural instability. Her core thesis: degraded cervical proprioceptive afferents create a sensory mismatch between cervical, vestibular, and visual systems, producing measurable disturbances in joint position sense, eye movement control, and postural stability — along with autonomic symptoms (lightheadedness, nausea, blurred vision, spatial disorientation) driven by aberrant signaling through the reticular formation and vestibulo-sympathetic reflex pathway.

The model is well-supported. The problem is its empirical base.

Treleaven's foundational research was conducted almost exclusively in **whiplash-associated disorder (WAD) populations**: acute traumatic onset, predominantly upper cervical involvement, typically single- or two-level structural disruption. This makes clinical sense — WAD is a common, well-defined research cohort with clear injury dating and consistent presentation.

But whiplash is not the only pathway to cervical sensorimotor dysfunction, and the patients who fall outside the WAD model are not well-served by it. Specifically:

- **Multi-level cervical degenerative disc disease** spanning five or more segments (e.g., C2–T1)
- **Degenerative rather than traumatic etiology**, with progressive mechanoreceptor degradation across years rather than acute disruption
- **Pre-existing compensation architectures** from prior surgeries or long-duration asymmetric loading
- **Elite athletic training histories** that have built deeply conditioned proprioceptive patterns — patterns the degeneration has now made unreliable

These patients present the same sensory mismatch mechanism Treleaven describes, but the **distribution, chronicity, and compensatory complexity** differ fundamentally from the WAD model. The question is whether these differences change the expected clinical behavior, therapeutic targeting, or trajectory.

## What Li et al. (2022) Does to v1

Li et al. closes the stated gap almost exactly: their pathophysiology section builds the same mismatch model — mechanoreceptor and nociceptor ingrowth into degenerated discs, inflammatory alteration of firing characteristics, erroneous afferents mismatching normal vestibular/visual input — on degenerative rather than traumatic foundations. v1's Gap section argued this pathway existed but was under-documented. Li documents it. The framing holds; the novelty claim narrows accordingly.

**Citation:** Li Y, Yang L, Dai C, Peng B. Proprioceptive Cervicogenic Dizziness: A Narrative Review of Pathogenesis, Diagnosis, and Treatment. *J Clin Med*. 2022;11(21):6293. doi:10.3390/jcm11216293

Two anatomical details worth naming precisely:

- **The intervertebral disc sits on the central axis of cervical motion.** Disc mechanoreceptors therefore occupy the most mechanically informative site in the spine. Six degenerated levels means six noise sources at the exact position where signal fidelity matters most.

- **Thunberg et al. (Li ref 22):** chemically sensitive facet joint afferents connect to fusimotor neurons of dorsal cervical muscles and can trigger positive feedback loops producing chronic pain and stiffness. That is a mechanism for emergent system-level behavior — noise at the joints amplifies spindle gain in the muscles, which generates more abnormal input. Sub-question 1 (aggregate vs. emergent) now has a plausible mechanism in the literature, though no one has tested it at this distribution.

## Two Revisions to v1's Mechanism

### 1. GTOs Restored to the Model

v1's working notes treated the Golgi tendon organ framing as yoga-anatomy folklore. Li et al. lists GTOs as full members of the cervical proprioceptive apparatus — afferents "primarily divided into three groups: joint receptors, muscle spindle, and Golgi tendon organs," with spindle and GTO information matching "in appropriate cervical alignment" (refs 14, 67). Abnormal curvature (this presentation includes reversal of lordosis) produces aberrant input from both. The rejection stands only against the folk version ("sustained stretch fires GTOs → muscle relaxes"). As force-sense organs participating in the alignment-matching computation, GTOs belong in the model.

### 2. The Scapular-Chain Mechanism, Restructured

v1 proposed the right scapular complex was upweighted as substitute proprioceptive data. Li's spindle-density data complicates that: upper trapezius has among the lowest spindle densities recorded (2.2/g, against 242/g in inferior oblique) (ref 16). Trapezius is a poor candidate for backup spindle source.

The compensation mechanism survives reframing. What the 25-year right-chain tension held was **alignment** — the curvature and resting tone context within which cervical spindles and GTOs calibrate. Li separately notes prolonged unilateral contraction significantly increases spindle sensitivity (ref 48). The defensible mechanism is indirect: the right chain maintained a cervical configuration the sensorimotor system had recalibrated around; releasing it abruptly changed the operating range of the spindles that remained, on top of dumping mechanical load onto degenerated segments.

v1's clinical observation stands. The mechanism sentence shifts from "backup proprioceptive input" to **"calibration-stabilizing tension"** — a stronger claim, because it is testable via the JPE protocol.

Li cannot account for the release-worsening phenomenon: their treatment section recommends manual therapy as the most-supported intervention and never considers that releasing compensatory tissue could destabilize a reweighted system. That remains genuinely novel territory.

## The Adverse Event as n=1 Evidence

See `neuro-storm-case-report.md` for full documentation. Summary for thesis purposes:

A gaze-fixation exercise from a concussion rehabilitation protocol — 60 seconds total, 3-second holds, minimal eccentricity — produced a neurological cascade lasting 72+ hours, converting to a flare extending beyond six days, with endocrine disruption (menstrual cycle triggered weeks early). The exercise demands sustained visual fixation at eccentric gaze positions while the head is held static: squarely inside Li's provocation zone ("symptoms reproduced by a specific neck movement or position rather than a whole-body movement") (refs 4, 70).

Li describes episodes lasting minutes to hours (ref 8). This ran 24+. That is not a contradiction — it is amplitude. A three-second hold producing a multi-day cascade is n=1 evidence that the afferent noise floor sits far outside the review's reference population. It converts "the exercise was too much" into "the exercise exposed how far outside the reference population the threshold sits."

## Why Concussion Protocols Structurally Fail Here

Li's justification for vestibular rehabilitation is specific: a well-integrated vestibulo-cerebellar system can **compensate** for changed cervical afferents (ref 101). Compensation — routing around a stable impairment.

Concussion vision protocols operate on a **correction** premise: retrain integration accuracy on the assumption that all channels are structurally intact and the central processor needs practice. For a brain with intact hardware, correction works. For a system whose transducers are histologically altered — mechanoreceptors that grew into degenerated disc tissue and fire erroneously — accuracy cannot be retrained out of the channel. One can compensate around it or reduce noise at the source.

Correction vs. compensation is the cleanest articulation of why every concussion-derived protocol handed to this presentation has failed, and anchors the treatment-section rewrite.

Li also documents the pharmacological version of noise reduction: antidepressants are hypothesized to work partly by inhibiting proprioceptive input from muscle spindle afferents (ref 14). Suppressing the noisy channel is recognized therapeutic logic. This connects to the depression comorbidity flagged in v1.

## SCED Design Upgrades

Three concrete upgrades to v1's experimental framework:

1. **Outcome battery is now literature-anchored.** JPE via laser headlamp, posturography, and the symptom constellation are Li's Table 3 diagnostic toolkit, with defined thresholds (JPE > 4.5° positive; specificity 75% / sensitivity 72%) (ref 73).

2. **Li's Box 1 diagnostic criteria provide an eligibility rubric.** This presentation satisfies A, B, and D; criterion C3 — two positive clinical tests — is exactly what the SCED baseline phase would generate. Baseline JPE and posturography are not just data collection; they complete a published diagnostic checklist on this presentation.

3. **The yoga hypothesis gains a mechanistic hook.** Li notes sympathetic outflow intensely inhibits cervical spindle input (ref 21), and that spindle function is modulated by autonomic/emotional state (refs 14, 57). Breath-linked autonomic downregulation concurrent with proprioceptive demand plausibly modulates spindle gain in real time, widening the tolerance window during loading. That is a testable mechanism.

One endpoint framed neutrally: Li documents refractory cases — including disc degeneration without cord or root compression — showing dizziness resolution after percutaneous disc decompression (refs 27, 64–66). An existence proof that reducing noise at its structural source can resolve the downstream cascade. A distant clinical contingency, not a recommendation.

## Remaining Novel Contributions (v2 Novelty Claim)

After Li, what remains genuinely unpublished:

1. **Compensation-release destabilization.** Releasing compensatory tissue can destabilize a reweighted sensorimotor system. Undocumented; Li recommends manual therapy without considering the risk.

2. **The 6–48 hour lag.** Li describes minutes-to-hours episodes (ref 8). Nothing in the literature explains multi-day delayed cascades.

3. **Trained proprioceptive history.** Elite-trained bodies with high-trust internal signals that become unreliable — untouched anywhere.

4. **The multi-level distribution question.** Mechanism now suggested (six noise sources at the central axis of cervical motion; Thunberg's fusimotor feedback loop), untested.

Those four are the contribution. The rest is scaffolding on top of Li et al., which enters the Key References list as the degenerative-etiology counterpart to Treleaven's WAD base — arguably the single most load-bearing citation for the gap argument.
