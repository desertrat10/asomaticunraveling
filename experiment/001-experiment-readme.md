# Experiment — Introduction

*Field notes from one person running a self-experiment on multi-level cervical degeneration and sensorimotor dysfunction*

---

## What This Is

This folder contains a patient-led research project on a specific kind of chronic neck problem that isn't well-covered in mainstream medical literature. The author has six degenerated cervical discs (C2–T1) that send corrupted position signals to the brain, producing dizziness, autonomic symptoms (nausea, lightheadedness), and delayed symptom cascades that unfold over days rather than minutes.

The core question: when standard rehabilitation protocols designed for traumatic neck injuries (whiplash, concussion) are applied to a degenerative multi-level case, what breaks—and what actually works instead?

This is **single-case self-experimentation** (n=1). Nothing here generalizes beyond this one body. It's offered as documentation for other patients and as a framework for researchers interested in sensorimotor dysfunction outside the whiplash-derived literature.

## Document Summaries

### [`working-thesis-v2.md`](working-thesis-v2-OCT2026.md) — The Main Thesis

This is the core research document. It starts with the observation that nearly all existing research on cervicogenic dizziness (dizziness caused by neck pathology) is based on **whiplash-associated disorder (WAD)** populations—young-ish people with acute traumatic injuries, typically one or two damaged disc levels. But many patients have **degenerative multi-level cervical disc disease**—progressive wear-and-tear across five or more levels, often starting in their 40s or 50s, sometimes decades after prior surgeries or asymmetric occupational loading.

Version 2 of this thesis was revised after discovering Li et al. (2022)—a comprehensive review that builds the same sensory-mismatch model on degenerative foundations, which months of literature searching had not surfaced. The thesis now distinguishes what Li documents (the degenerative pathway exists, the mechanism is understood) from what remains genuinely novel: compensation-release destabilization, the 6–48 hour symptom lag, trained proprioceptive history as a clinical variable, and the multi-level distribution question. The experimental design (SCED) is also upgraded to use literature-anchored diagnostic tools.

**Who should read this:** Researchers, clinicians, or patients who want the full theoretical framework before diving into specifics.

### [`neuro-storm-case-report.md`](neuro-storm-case-report.md) — The Adverse Event

On approximately October 3, 2026, a 60-second gaze-fixation exercise from a concussion rehabilitation protocol triggered a neurological cascade that lasted 72+ hours, with the flare extending beyond six days as of documentation. The exercise—fixating on a thumb held at varying angles—seemed innocuous. In a healthy vestibular system, it's negligible. In a system where six degenerated discs are sending corrupted position signals, it overloaded the mismatch resolution center (vestibular nuclei) and recruited the vestibulo-sympathetic reflex pathway, producing nausea, spatial disorientation, vision blurring, and—unexpectedly—endocrine disruption (menstrual cycle triggered weeks early).

This case report maps the cascade onto Li et al.'s documented circuitry, explains why concussion protocols are structurally mismatched to degenerative DDD presentations, and extracts implications for future experimental design: lower provocation thresholds, narrower safe-dose windows, and expanded endpoint tracking (endocrine markers, sleep architecture, not just HRV).

**Who should read this:** Anyone who wants to understand what went wrong when the "wrong tool" was applied—and what that reveals about the underlying condition.

### [`model-access-notes.md`](model-access-notes.md) — The Search Story

Between April and October 2026, repeated literature searches were conducted using AI-assisted research tools to find papers on degenerative multi-level cervical dizziness. The retrieval landscape was dominated by whiplash literature—Treleaven's WAD-based models, traumatic injury cohorts, upper cervical focus. The Li et al. (2022) review—a comprehensive degenerative-etiology counterpart published in the Journal of Clinical Medicine—never surfaced, despite being open access, PubMed-indexed, and directly relevant.

After the neuro storm incident, a search through a different model ecosystem (open-weights rather than frontier) immediately found Li et al. and a cluster of related work from Chinese and Japanese spine research groups. This document examines candidate explanations (training data geography, query-semantics lock-in, ecosystem diversity) and draws implications for patient-led research: retrieve redundantly across tools, search outside dominant paradigms' vocabulary, mine references backward once you land one hit, and treat "nothing exists" claims from any single retrieval pathway as provisional.

**Who should read this:** Patients doing self-directed medical research, anyone studying AI-assisted literature review limitations, or skeptics wondering why the gap claim existed in the first place.
