# Beyond Whiplash: Sensorimotor Dysfunction in Multi-Level Cervical Degenerative Disc Disease

*A research premise, experimental question, and proposed investigative framework*

---

## The Gap

Julia Treleaven's sensorimotor model (2008) is the most coherent framework available for explaining how cervical spine pathology produces dizziness, autonomic disruption, and postural instability. Her core thesis: degraded cervical proprioceptive afferents create a sensory mismatch between cervical, vestibular, and visual systems, producing measurable disturbances in joint position sense, eye movement control, and postural stability — along with autonomic symptoms (lightheadedness, nausea, blurred vision, spatial disorientation) driven by aberrant signaling through the reticular formation and vestibulo-sympathetic reflex pathway.

The model is well-supported. The problem is its empirical base.

Treleaven's foundational research was conducted almost exclusively in **whiplash-associated disorder (WAD) populations**: acute traumatic onset, predominantly upper cervical involvement, typically single- or two-level structural disruption. This makes clinical sense — WAD is a common, well-defined research cohort with clear injury dating and consistent presentation.

But whiplash is not the only pathway to cervical sensorimotor dysfunction, and the patients who fall outside the WAD model are not well-served by it. Specifically:

- **Multi-level cervical degenerative disc disease** spanning five or more segments (e.g., C2–T1)
- **Degenerative rather than traumatic etiology**, with progressive mechanoreceptor degradation across years rather than acute disruption
- **Pre-existing compensation architectures** from prior surgeries or long-duration asymmetric loading
- **Elite athletic training histories** that have built deeply conditioned proprioceptive patterns — patterns the degeneration has now made unreliable

These patients present the same sensory mismatch mechanism Treleaven describes, but the **distribution, chronicity, and compensatory complexity** differ fundamentally from the WAD model. The question is whether these differences change the expected clinical behavior, therapeutic targeting, or trajectory.

## The Case

**Patient profile:**
- Early 50s, former Division I collegiate athlete (scholarship, elite-level training history), former career requiring decades of asymmetric heavy gear carrying, certified yoga therapist (500-hour advanced certification, 15+ years of personal practice)
- Prior upper extremity surgery over two decades ago — never fully recovered biomechanically; the contralateral side has carried compensatory load across upper trap, levator scap, rhomboids, and thoracic erectors for the full intervening period
- Cervical spine injury approximately 29 months ago
- Currently in second round of PT, transitioning from reactive release toward endurance-based strengthening

**Structural findings (2023 MRI):**
- Reversal of normal cervical lordosis
- Multi-level degenerative disc disease: mild C2–C3, moderate C3–C4, moderate C4–C5, moderate C5–C6, **severe C6–C7**, mild C7–T1
- C4–C5 left subarticular disc extrusion
- **Severe left C5–C6 neural foraminal narrowing**
- **Mild C6–C7 central canal stenosis**
- Multilevel moderate neural foraminal narrowing
- First cervical MRI from approximately two decades prior provides a long-term structural baseline

**Sensorimotor presentation consistent with Treleaven's model:**
- Positional lightheadedness (post-PT, during flares)
- Blurred/soft vision (post-PT, during flares)
- Spatial disorientation during postural transitions
- Brain fog correlated with autonomic constellation
- Nausea during sustained autonomic activation
- 6–48 hour delay between triggering activity and symptom onset
- Inability to accurately perceive cumulative load in real time

**The complication Treleaven's model doesn't account for:**

The nervous system's compensatory strategy has been to **upweight contralateral muscle spindle data** — from a scapular complex that has carried over two decades of compensation for the prior surgical deficit — as a substitute proprioceptive input for degraded cervical mechanoreceptor signaling. When current PT releases this tissue (the clinically appropriate intervention), it produces **larger autonomic responses** than seen in the prior PT round, because the tissue being released isn't just locally overworked — it's carrying backup proprioceptive responsibility for the entire cervical spine.

This is sensory reweighting operating at a scale and duration Treleaven's whiplash cohorts don't represent.

## The Question

**Does multi-level cervical degenerative disc disease produce a qualitatively different pattern of sensorimotor dysfunction than the whiplash-derived model predicts — and does that difference change therapeutic targeting?**

Sub-questions:

1. **Distribution**: When afferent corruption is distributed across six cervical levels rather than concentrated at one or two, does the sensory mismatch behave as an aggregate (sum of individual-level deficits) or as a system-level disruption with emergent properties the single-level model doesn't predict?

2. **Compensation architecture**: When decades of pre-existing compensation (from prior surgery, asymmetric occupational loading) have already reorganized the sensorimotor map before the cervical degeneration begins, does the central nervous system have fewer adaptive pathways available — and does this explain the larger autonomic responses observed when compensatory tissue is released?

3. **Trained proprioceptive history**: In bodies with elite-level proprioceptive training (Division I athletics, 15+ years of yoga practice with advanced certification and formal training), the internal feedback system has been conditioned into high sensitivity and high trust. When that system becomes unreliable due to cervical degeneration, does the **mismatch between trained proprioceptive confidence and actual signal degradation** produce a distinct clinical pattern — one where the patient is more likely to override external structure because the internal signal "feels" authoritative even when it's corrupt?

4. **Therapeutic pathway**: If the dysfunction is distributed and layered rather than focal and acute, does treatment need to address **proprioceptive recalibration across the full kinetic chain** rather than isolated cervical-level intervention — and does yoga therapy, with its integration of proprioceptive awareness, autonomic regulation, breath-linked movement, and interoceptive training, offer a therapeutic mechanism that standard PT protocols (designed for focal rehabilitation) do not?

## The Experimental Premise

### Hypothesis

Yoga therapy — specifically, sequences designed to systematically retrain cervical proprioception, scapular stabilization, autonomic regulation, and interoceptive awareness within a single integrated practice — produces measurable improvements in sensorimotor outcomes (joint position error, postural sway, autonomic markers) in multi-level cervical DDD that standard PT alone does not achieve, because it addresses the distributed and layered nature of the dysfunction rather than treating individual structural levels or muscle groups in isolation.

### Why yoga therapy, mechanistically

Treleaven's own treatment recommendations include "tailored sensorimotor control programs" that address head-eye coordination, cervical proprioception retraining, and balance. These are typically delivered as exercise prescriptions — discrete drills targeting individual deficits.

Yoga therapy operates differently. A well-designed therapeutic sequence:

- **Links proprioceptive demand to breath regulation** — engaging autonomic downregulation simultaneously with sensorimotor challenge, rather than treating them as separate systems
- **Progresses through postural transitions** — the exact context where spatial disorientation occurs — rather than training static positions
- **Builds interoceptive discrimination** — the capacity to detect internal state changes in real time, which is precisely the capacity cervical degeneration has compromised
- **Operates across the full kinetic chain** — scapular positioning, thoracic extension, cervical alignment, hip stability — rather than isolating the cervical segment
- **Trains the stopping signal** — the capacity to notice accumulation before the proprioceptive system reports it, using breath rate, effort quality, and attentional focus as proxy indicators for load the cervical mechanoreceptors can no longer accurately report

This isn't yoga as stretching. It's yoga as **sensorimotor recalibration infrastructure** — a system-level intervention for a system-level dysfunction.

### Proposed design: Single-Case Experimental Design (SCED)

A single-case experimental design (n=1, A-B-A or A-B-A-B with follow-up) using the patient as her own control, with repeated measures across baseline, intervention, and withdrawal phases.

**Primary outcome measures** (mapped to Treleaven's assessment domains):
- **Joint position error (JPE)**: Cervical relocation accuracy, measured via headlamp laser wall-target protocol at standardized distances. Multiple-direction testing (rotation L/R, flexion, extension)
- **Postural sway / balance**: Modified Clinical Test of Sensory Interaction in Balance (mCTSIB) — eyes open/closed, firm/foam surface conditions
- **Autonomic markers**: Heart rate variability (HRV), resting heart rate trend, autonomic symptom frequency and severity from daily structured logging

**Secondary measures**:
- Daily pain tracking (NRS)
- Multi-region body scan (directional/band language, daily)
- Autonomic symptom constellation tracking (positional lightheadedness, blurred vision, spatial disorientation, brain fog, nausea — binary presence + severity)
- Flare frequency, severity classification, and recovery window duration
- PT session response tracking (recalibration window duration and intensity)

**Intervention**: Yoga therapy sequences designed and refined by the patient-practitioner — a certified yoga therapist with advanced training, 15+ years of personal practice, and direct experiential knowledge of the condition being studied. Sequences target cervical proprioceptive recalibration, scapular stabilization, thoracic mobility, autonomic regulation, and interoceptive training. Specific sequences documented, versioned, and tracked for dose/frequency/modification.

**Data infrastructure**: Daily structured logs, weekly pattern reviews, flare records with trigger analysis, session-level response tracking. All data patient-generated, patient-held, and longitudinally continuous.

> *This is a single-case self-experimentation report. All findings are specific to one body, one condition, one set of protocols. Nothing here generalizes past n=1. Clinical decisions should be made with qualified providers, not from this document.*

## What This Asks of a Collaborator

This is not a request for treatment. The care team is in place and managing the condition actively.

This is an invitation to think about the question: **what happens when Treleaven's model meets a body it wasn't designed to describe** — one with distributed degenerative involvement, decades of compensation, elite proprioceptive training, and a therapeutic tool (yoga therapy) that addresses the system-level dysfunction the model identifies but the existing treatment literature doesn't fully explore?

The patient-practitioner brings:
- Over two decades of structural imaging history providing a long-term baseline
- Months of structured daily longitudinal data
- Designed and tested yoga therapy sequences with documented protocols
- A single-case experimental design framework ready for refinement
- Deep familiarity with both the clinical research and the lived experience of the condition

The collaborator brings:
- Clinical interpretation of the structural progression
- Perspective on whether the degenerative multi-level presentation differs clinically from the WAD populations the research is built on
- Network access to relevant specialists or research faculty who might be interested in the question
- Institutional knowledge of where this kind of inquiry finds a home
